## What the feature does

The module adds two kinds of practice tabs, ported from the Intro to Pharmacology drill (`exam1-drill/src/app.js`: Pair up, the drug ↔ receptor drill and the poisoning table drill).

- **Pair up.** The student moves chips into the boxes they belong to, either by dragging or by tapping a chip and then a box. A set is either one chip per box (receptor → what an agonist does there) or several chips per box (receptor → G protein). Check marks every chip. The wrong and unplaced chips come first, each with where it was put, where it belongs and why. "Retry the wrong ones" sends only those chips back to the tray. A set of 9 pairs or fewer is served whole; a bigger set gives 7 pairs a round, or 12 chips when several chips share a box. All three numbers are module options.
- **Table recall: one tab per course table.** Each mode asks one column of a row and wants another column as the answer, for example the poison → its antidote, or the drug → its receptor line. The student answers with one tap. The other answers come from the other rows of the table. A mode can put the row's own near miss first (the same receptor with the action flipped), take answers from the same section first, and leave out rows that would also be right (another drug with the same receptor and action). A miss comes back at the end of the round and leads the next round. The best first-round score per mode is kept. "Cover and reveal" shows the whole table with chosen columns covered, each covered cell opened by a Show button, plus Show all and Hide again.
- The best scores and the misses are kept in the saved progress (`DB.pairup`), so Export, Import and Erase cover them.

**How this differs from the core's `match` type.** `match` is a question in the bank: one stem with a few left and right items, graded all or nothing, recorded in the answer history, scheduled, and drawn onto exam papers. This module adds none of that and leaves `match` unchanged. Its answers are not bank answers and the scheduler does not see them, as in Intro to Pharmacology. It is separate practice over the course's own tables. Pair up's sets are bigger than a bank item (14 to 32 pairs), are served in rounds, can put several chips in one box, explain each chip, and retry only the wrong ones. Table recall asks every row of a table, one tap per row, with answers drawn from the other rows.
